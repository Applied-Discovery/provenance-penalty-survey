## Question

A TV program described a US case in which police, told that a missing man might be buried in his old garden, asked the home's new owners for permission to dig, and they agreed. What if the owners had refused? Could the police get a search warrant even though the owners are clearly not suspects? And if the owners had something to hide in the garden, like marijuana plants, could they let the police in without getting into trouble for the plants?

## Answer

If the owners refused, police could very likely get a warrant. A search warrant does not require that the property owner be a suspect; it requires probable cause that evidence of a crime is at a particular place. A credible, corroborated tip that a body is buried in the yard would usually satisfy a judge, and the owners' innocence would not prevent the digging. The warrant would, however, be limited to the scope the judge authorizes, such as excavating specific areas.

As for the marijuana, there is no reliable way to invite police in while shielding illegal plants. Officers lawfully on the property may seize anything illegal in plain view. Owners can limit consent to a particular area, but that only helps if the plants are not visible along the way. Practically, the options are to remove the plants first, or to decline consent and let police proceed under a warrant, whose scope will be defined by the court rather than by the owners.
