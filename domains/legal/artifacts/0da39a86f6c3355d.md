## Question

A UPS email disclaimer says: "UPS, the UPS brandmark, and the color brown are trademarks of United Parcel Service of America, Inc." What does it mean for a color to be a trademark? Does it only cover the brown in their logo? And would anyone using that color be infringing?

## Answer

Yes, within limits. Trademark law protects anything that identifies the source of goods or services, and a color can qualify once the public has come to associate it with one company. UPS has spent decades using its particular shade of brown on trucks, uniforms and packaging, so consumers see that brown and think UPS. The registration covers that specific shade, used in connection with delivery and logistics services, not the color brown in general. It doesn't stop you from painting your house brown, selling brown shoes, or using brown in an unrelated business. Infringement requires a likelihood of confusion: another shipping or courier company dressing its drivers and vans in a similar brown could be liable, because customers might mistake it for UPS. Outside that context, using brown is perfectly legal, and UPS would have no claim.
