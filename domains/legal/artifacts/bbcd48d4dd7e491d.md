## Question

In the US, if someone buys something with a credit card and then doesn't pay the bill, can the card issuer claim ownership of what was bought? Or is it just a dispute over the unpaid debt?

## Answer

Generally, no. A typical credit card purchase is an unsecured loan. The issuer pays the merchant, ownership of the item passes to you at the sale, and what you owe the issuer is simply money. The card company has no lien on or title to the goods, so it cannot demand the item back or claim it as its own; the matter is a debt dispute. Its remedies are late fees, interest, closing the account, reporting to credit bureaus, and eventually suing for the balance. If it wins a judgment, it can collect through wage garnishment or a levy on your bank account or property, which could theoretically reach the item, but only as ordinary assets, not as something it owns. The main exception is certain store-branded cards or retail installment agreements whose terms grant the retailer a security interest in purchased goods, allowing repossession, though this is rarely pursued in practice.
