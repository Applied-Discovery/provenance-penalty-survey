## Question

I was hired on a 1099 basis to write software for a client in the US. I drafted a contract covering the work, the price and ownership, but neither of us ever signed it. I did the work and was paid in full anyway. Who owns the software?

## Answer

Under U.S. copyright law, the person who actually writes software owns the copyright the moment it is created, unless one of two exceptions applies. The first is work done by an employee within the scope of employment, which does not cover an independent contractor paid on a 1099 basis. The second is a signed written agreement that transfers ownership or designates the work as made for hire, and contractor-written software often does not even fit the made-for-hire categories. Since nothing was signed, you almost certainly still own the copyright. Because the client paid in full for software built for his purposes, he very likely holds an implied license to use it, probably an irrevocable one, but he cannot claim ownership or prevent you from reusing the code. To head off a dispute, consider offering a short written license or assignment now.
