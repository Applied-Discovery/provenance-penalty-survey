## Question

I live in a shared house where each bedroom has a lock. I keep finding keys in communal areas, such as at the bottom of cupboards, that look like they fit the bedroom doors. Would it be legal to try them in other rooms' doors? I want to know if the landlord is leaving tenants' keys lying around, since then a key to my room could be too. I'd normally ask, but several rooms are vacant and other tenants are away.

## Answer

Don't do it. Another tenant's locked bedroom is their private living space, and you have no right to open it without their permission, even if you found the key and don't plan to step inside. Opening the door, or even simply working the lock, could be treated as trespass or as unlawfully entering a dwelling, and if anything later goes missing or is damaged you would be the obvious suspect. Your reason for testing the keys would not be a defence. The safer route is to test found keys only on your own door, which you have already done, and then raise the issue with the landlord in writing, asking who has keys to your room and whether the loose keys work on it. If you remain worried, ask for your lock to be changed; your province's residential tenancy office can explain your rights and how to compel that.
