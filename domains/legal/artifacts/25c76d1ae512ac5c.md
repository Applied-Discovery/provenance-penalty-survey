## Question

My lease has switched to month-to-month, but my landlord admits he made a mistake and needs me out. We've agreed on a sum he'll pay me to move out at the end of this month. Because I haven't given one month's written notice, I would technically be breaking the law by leaving. Is there a form we can sign, such as an agreement not to sue, since we've resolved this between ourselves?

## Answer

You are not breaking the law by leaving without a month's written notice if the landlord agrees. The notice requirement protects the landlord, and a landlord can waive it. What you want is a written mutual agreement to end the tenancy, not a covenant not to sue. Most provinces have a standard form for exactly this, usually available from the provincial residential tenancy office, and using it is the simplest option. Whether you use the form or write your own, it should state the move-out date, the amount the landlord will pay you and when, how your security deposit will be handled, and that both of you release each other from any further claims arising from the tenancy. Both sign and date it, and each keeps a copy. Get the payment before or when you hand over the keys.
