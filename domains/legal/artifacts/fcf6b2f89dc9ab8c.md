## Question

On the TV show *Cops*, people who run from the police often say it was because they had a warrant. Why aren't all people with warrants arrested immediately? Can't the police just go to their house and pick them up? Or are these people all homeless or hiding?

## Answer

There are far more outstanding warrants than officers available to serve them. Many large counties have tens or hundreds of thousands of open warrants, most for minor matters like unpaid fines, missed court dates, or probation violations. Police agencies prioritize the serious ones, such as violent felonies, and the rest simply sit in a database until the person happens to be stopped for something else. Even when officers want to serve a warrant, the address on file is often outdated, the person may have moved, or nobody answers the door, and officers generally cannot force entry into a home on a minor warrant without confirming the person is inside. Many people with warrants also stay with friends or move frequently, which makes them hard to locate. So most warrants are resolved incidentally, during traffic stops and street encounters like the ones you see on television.
