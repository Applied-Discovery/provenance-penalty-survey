## Question

A colleague filmed a sporting event on his phone and sent me the video. I entered it in a TV competition and it won AU$1,000. I suggested we split the prize, but he wants all of it, saying he owns the video. Is he entitled to the whole prize?

## Answer

Your colleague almost certainly owns the video. Under Australian copyright law, the person who makes a film owns the copyright in it, and a phone recording counts as a film. Simply sending you a copy does not transfer ownership; that requires a written and signed assignment. At most he gave you an implied permission to watch or share it, not to enter it in a competition for money.

Whether he is entitled to the prize is a separate question. The prize was awarded to you as the entrant under the program's competition terms, so he has no automatic legal right to the money. However, if you entered his work without consent, you likely breached those terms, which usually require you to own or have permission to use the footage, and the program could withdraw the prize. He could also claim compensation for infringement. Given that risk, a negotiated split is a sensible outcome.
