## Question

A close friend runs a small family restaurant as a sole proprietor, and she treats her two workers as independent contractors. I'm fairly sure the IRS would reclassify them as employees and fine her if it expanded its audit. I'm advising her on her options. I also helped her on a phone call with the IRS about another audit, and the agent took down my name. I'm unpaid, unlicensed and not a lawyer, and she can't afford professional advice. Can I get into trouble for this, for example under the Trust Fund Recovery Penalty?

## Answer

Giving unpaid, informal advice to a friend is generally not illegal. The rules on who may practice before the IRS mainly target people who charge for preparing returns or formally represent taxpayers, and speaking on a call while your friend was present and authorized you is normally fine. The Trust Fund Recovery Penalty is aimed at people who control a business's finances and choose to pay other bills instead of remitting withheld payroll taxes; a friend offering tips is not a "responsible person." Your real exposure is different: if your advice is wrong she has no recourse against you, and if you knowingly help her conceal the misclassification or evade tax, that can be a crime. Stick to explaining options, urge her to fix the classification going forward, and point her to the IRS voluntary classification settlement program, an enrolled agent, or a low-income taxpayer clinic.
