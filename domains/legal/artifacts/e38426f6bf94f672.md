## Question

Canary Wharf in London is a privately owned public space. Security has already caught me walking on the grass and jumping on a stone wall as part of my workout, and I'm worried I'll eventually be banned. Can they ban me? Do they have authority over a sovereign man? Does it matter whether it's their private land, or a public space on their private land? And what exactly is their private security allowed to do?

## Answer

Yes, you can be banned. The Canary Wharf estate is private land, and the public is there only with the owner's permission. That permission can be withdrawn from anyone, at any time, for any reason or none, and the "sovereign man" idea has no legal standing whatsoever. Once told you are no longer welcome, you become a trespasser if you return. Trespass is generally a civil matter rather than a crime, so you would not normally be prosecuted for entering, but the owner can ask you to leave, have security use reasonable force to remove you, and seek a court injunction. Breaching an injunction is contempt of court and can lead to a fine or imprisonment. Security staff have no special powers beyond those of the landowner and any citizen: they cannot detain you except in the narrow circumstances of a citizen's arrest. Any genuine public rights of way crossing the estate would remain open to you.
