## Question

In the UK, suppose a landlord runs up £50,000 in costs evicting a bad tenant, and the court rules that the tenant owes him that amount. Can he sell the debt to a collection agency, and would any agency realistically want to buy it?

## Answer

Yes. Once the court has given judgment, the £50,000 becomes a judgment debt, and a judgment debt is a form of property that can be assigned to someone else. Mr Jones would sign a written assignment transferring the debt to the collection agency and give the tenant written notice of it, and the agency could then enforce the judgment in its own name, asking the court to record it as the new creditor where needed.

Whether anyone would buy it is a different matter. Agencies price debts on the likelihood of recovery, not the face value. An evicted tenant who has just cost his landlord £50,000 will often have no property, savings or steady income, so a buyer might offer only a few pence in the pound, or nothing at all. Mr Jones may do better instructing an agency or enforcement officers on a commission basis, or enforcing the judgment himself.
