## Question

In the US, information covered by non-disclosure agreements increasingly appears in newspapers. Why can't the newspaper be held liable for publishing it?

## Answer

An NDA is a contract, and a contract binds only the people who signed it. The newspaper never agreed to keep anything secret, so it cannot be sued for breaching a promise it never made. The person who leaked the information can be sued, and that is where the legal risk falls.

Beyond contract law, the First Amendment gives publishers strong protection when they print truthful information about matters of public concern that they obtained lawfully, even if someone else broke a promise or a law to pass it along. Courts almost never allow the government to block publication in advance, and they rarely impose damages after the fact.

A newspaper can face liability if it actively induced the breach, paid for stolen documents, or knowingly misappropriated trade secrets, but simply receiving and publishing leaked material is generally not enough.
