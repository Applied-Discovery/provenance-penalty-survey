## Question

Under US law, would a ticket or employment contract for a one-way trip to Mars be legal, given that everyone knows the astronaut will die there, if not before? Or would it run into the limits of contract law and issues like euthanasia?

## Answer

Nothing in US law forbids agreeing to a one-way trip, even one that will end in death far from home. People lawfully take on extremely dangerous work, and a contract for an expedition with no return is not itself illegal or a form of euthanasia, since nobody is agreeing to be killed; the danger comes from the environment, not the other party. Courts would, however, scrutinize the deal closely. The traveler's informed consent must be genuine, and any clause waiving the company's liability for its own negligence or recklessness may be unenforceable, especially where a death results. Federal rules already require commercial spaceflight participants to receive written risk disclosures and sign informed consent forms, and the launch itself needs government licensing. So the ticket is likely legal, but the company cannot contract away all responsibility.
