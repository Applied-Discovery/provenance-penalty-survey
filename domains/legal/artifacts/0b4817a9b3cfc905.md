## Question

At what point in a fraudulent transaction does fraud happen? When the fraudulent good or service is offered, when it's paid for, or at some other moment?

## Answer

In California, the answer depends on which kind of fraud you mean. For a civil fraud claim, the wrong is not complete until the victim actually relies on the false statement and suffers a loss, so simply offering a bogus product or service is not enough; the fraud happens when money or property changes hands, or the victim otherwise acts to their detriment. For the crime of theft by false pretenses, the offense is likewise complete only when the victim, relying on the deception, hands over money or property. However, making the dishonest offer with intent to defraud can already be charged as an attempt, even if nobody pays. Separately, false or misleading advertising is unlawful the moment it is made, regardless of whether anyone buys. So the offer can create liability, but the classic fraud occurs at the moment of payment.
