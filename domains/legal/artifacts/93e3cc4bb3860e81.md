## Question

I got a speeding ticket in Texas (73 in a 55). The offense was on March 2nd, but the officer wrote March 3rd on the ticket. The court says it can amend the ticket and send me a new copy. If the officer signed off on a wrong date, couldn't the rest be wrong too? Is this grounds for dismissal, is the amendment legal, or should I just pay since I'd lose in court?

## Answer

Yes, this is legal, and a wrong date is unlikely to get the ticket dismissed. In Texas, a traffic citation is essentially a notice to appear; the actual charging document is the complaint the prosecutor files with the court, and the prosecutor can amend that complaint to correct clerical mistakes like a date, usually as long as you get proper notice before trial. Courts routinely allow this, so arguing that the whole ticket is unreliable because of one typo will not persuade a judge on its own.

That said, the error is not useless to you. You could use it at trial to question the officer's attention to detail, and you can still challenge the radar or pacing evidence. Practically, consider asking about deferred disposition or a defensive driving course, which can keep the violation off your record, rather than simply paying it.
