## Question

I'm 17 and on holiday. Yesterday I started "training" at a new tea shop, working 9:30am to 7:30pm on setup, the till, drinks, cleaning and closing. I've signed up for six more days in a row, but the manager says none of it is paid. I'll only get the national minimum wage of £8.00 an hour after 80 hours of training and an onboarding test. My co-workers also did unpaid training. Is this legal in the UK, and how should I respond to her?

## Answer

No, this is almost certainly not legal. Once you are doing real work for the business, such as running the till, making drinks and cleaning, you are a worker and must be paid at least the minimum wage for your age for every hour, including time spent training and on any required test. A short unpaid trial can be lawful only if it is genuinely an assessment, not ten-hour shifts across a week. That your co-workers accepted the same arrangement does not make it lawful.

Respond politely in writing, saying you understand the law requires payment for all hours worked, including training, and asking to be paid for the shifts done so far and any further ones. Keep a record of your hours. If she refuses, contact Acas for free advice, and you can report the shop to HMRC, which enforces the minimum wage and can recover arrears for you.
