# Public money should be raised through taxation rather than national lotteries.

A lottery is a tax that falls hardest on those who can least afford it. The people who buy the most tickets are not the comfortable but the hard-pressed, drawn in by the promise of a way out. When a hospital wing or a sports ground is paid for from their losses, the poorest are quietly funding services that everyone enjoys, while the well-off contribute nothing. Taxation, by contrast, is set openly and asks more of those with more.

There is also the question of honesty. A lottery dresses up a levy as a flutter, so nobody has to defend the spending it pays for. Taxes are debated, budgeted and answerable at elections. If a good cause deserves public money, we should be willing to vote for it, not hope the desperate will pay.
