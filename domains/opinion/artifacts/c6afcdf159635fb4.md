# Parents should not tell children that Santa Claus is real.

Children trust their parents more than anyone, and that trust is the foundation of every later lesson about honesty. Telling a child that Santa Claus is real, and then defending the story with fake footprints and half-eaten mince pies, spends that trust on a game the child never agreed to play. Many children remember learning the truth as their first discovery that adults will lie with a straight face.

None of this requires giving up the magic. A child can hang a stocking, leave out a carrot and delight in the story of Father Christmas while knowing it is a story, just as they enjoy dragons and talking rabbits. Shared make-believe is joyful; deliberate deception of a small person who cannot see through it is not.
