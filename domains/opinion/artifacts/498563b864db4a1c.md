# Pre-nuptial agreements should be banned.

Marriage is meant to be a shared undertaking, yet a pre-nuptial agreement asks one partner to plan for its failure before it has even begun. That is not prudence; it is a hedge that quietly favours whoever already holds the money. The wealthier partner arrives with lawyers and leverage, while the other signs under the pressure of a wedding date and the fear of seeming mercenary. Consent given in those circumstances is rarely free.

Courts already have the tools to divide property fairly when a marriage ends, weighing each person's contribution, including years spent raising children or supporting a career. Private contracts that override that judgement let the strong opt out of fairness. Banning them would restore the principle that marriage is a partnership of equals.
