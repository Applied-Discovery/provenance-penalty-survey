# Latin and Ancient Greek should be taught in schools.

Latin and Ancient Greek are not dead languages but the foundations of the ones we speak. A pupil who knows that "aqua" means water and "graph" means writing can decode thousands of English words on sight, from aqueduct to autobiography. Learning to parse a Latin sentence teaches grammar more clearly than any lesson in English can, because every ending must be accounted for.

The classics also open a door to the history, philosophy and literature that shaped Europe's laws, medicine and politics. Reading Homer or Cicero in the original is a discipline in patience and precision. Schools that drop these subjects narrow horizons and leave the classics to the privileged few.
