# Museums should be free to visit.

Museums hold what a nation already owns in common: its paintings, fossils, machines and manuscripts, cared for at public expense. Charging at the door turns a shared inheritance into a ticketed attraction, and the people it turns away are precisely those who would gain most. A family on a tight budget will not gamble twenty pounds on an afternoon the children may not enjoy; a student cannot drop in for one gallery on the way home. Britain's experiment with free national museums shows what happens when the barrier goes: visitor numbers rose sharply, and repeat visits became ordinary. Free entry is the cheapest way to make culture genuinely public.
