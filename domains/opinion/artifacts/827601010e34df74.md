# It is dangerous to teach history in schools.

History lessons rarely teach history; they teach a story. Somebody chooses which wars count as glorious, which leaders as heroes and which peoples barely get a mention, and that somebody is usually a government. Children then absorb the chosen version before they can question it. Nations across Europe and Asia still quarrel over textbooks precisely because everyone knows what a classroom can plant in a young mind. A syllabus that flatters the state breeds resentment of neighbours and an easy pride that curdles into nationalism. Better to leave the past to adults who can weigh competing accounts than to hand it to children as settled fact.
