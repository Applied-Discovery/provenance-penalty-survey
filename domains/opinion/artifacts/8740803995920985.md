# The school-leaving age should be raised.

Sixteen is too young to leave education for good. The jobs that once absorbed early leavers, in factories, mines and shipyards, have largely gone, and the work that remains increasingly demands qualifications, whether a plumber's certificate or a nursing degree. Those who leave at sixteen are far more likely to be unemployed at twenty-five and to earn less for the rest of their lives, at a cost to them and to the taxpayer. Raising the leaving age to eighteen need not mean two more years of classroom lessons: apprenticeships, technical colleges and part-time study all count. What matters is that no young person drifts out of learning before they have something to show for it.
