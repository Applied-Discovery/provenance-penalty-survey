# Everyone should automatically be opted in to organ donation.

Thousands of people die each year waiting for a transplant, yet surveys consistently show that most of us would happily donate our organs after death. The problem is not unwillingness but paperwork. Few people get round to registering, and grieving families, unsure of a loved one's wishes, often refuse. An opt-out system fixes this mismatch. Anyone with an objection, religious or otherwise, can still remove themselves with a simple form, so nobody's freedom is taken away. Wales moved to presumed consent in 2015 and saw consent rates rise noticeably, and Spain, which has long presumed consent, leads the world in donations. When the default reflects what most people actually want, and costs the dissenting minority a few minutes, it is the humane choice.
