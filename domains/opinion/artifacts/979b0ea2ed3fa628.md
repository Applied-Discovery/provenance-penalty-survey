# Spelling for the English language should be reformed to be simpler.

English spelling is a historical accident, not a design. Words like though, through, tough and thought share letters but not sounds, and children spend years memorising exceptions that carry no meaning. Countries that reformed their spelling, such as Germany, Norway and Turkey, did not lose their literature; they gained faster literacy. English-speaking children take two to three years longer to read fluently than Finnish or Italian children, whose spelling matches speech.

Reform need not be sudden or total. Dropping silent letters and settling on one spelling for each common sound would remove the worst irregularities while keeping texts recognisable. The cost of change is paid once; the cost of the current muddle is paid by every learner, every year.
