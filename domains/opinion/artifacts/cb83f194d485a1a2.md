# Reality television has a negative effect on society.

Reality television thrives on conflict, humiliation and manufactured drama, and it teaches audiences that these are the ordinary stuff of human life. Programmes such as Love Island or The Apprentice reward contestants for scheming, shouting and betraying one another, while quieter virtues go unfilmed. Viewers absorb the message that fame is a prize worth any indignity and that appearance matters more than character.

Participants are often cast for their vulnerabilities, edited into villains and then left to face public abuse without support; several have taken their own lives. Cheap formats also crowd out drama, documentary and journalism that inform rather than provoke. A culture fed on staged spite grows coarser, and reality television is a chief supplier.
